Κοινή — the everyday tongue

Everyday Koine

Learn to read the Greek New Testament, a few minutes at a time. Chapters that teach the grammar, recorded to be read aloud, vocabulary with audio on a schedule that brings each word back just as you are about to forget it, and the whole Greek text with every word parsed.

Open the app

It runs in your browser: no account, no ads, and it works offline once opened.

What is in it

27 chapters

From the letters to reading the text, in short parts with questions along the way. Every chapter will have audio: a voice reads it to you, tables and verses included, lighting each word as it is spoken. About a quarter of the chapters have it so far.

818 words, with audio

The New Testament’s commonest words, down to about fifteen occurrences — nine words in ten of the running text — each with a recording and an example verse, on a spaced-repetition schedule.

Paradigms you can play

28 reference tables, 20 of them as fill-in-the-grid rounds against the clock, and a parsing drill marked against 1,477 real forms from the text.

The whole Greek New Testament

Every word parsed and glossed on a tap, dimmed or lit by what you already know, plus 12 graded passages and 630 sentence questions drawn from the text.

How a day goes

Open it and Today tells you what to do: the words the schedule has brought back, a few new ones, and the next part of the chapter you are on. Most days that is ten minutes or so. A wrong answer is not a failure — it just brings the word back sooner.

Accurate by construction

An app that teaches a language makes claims about it, and a confident wrong answer is worse than none. So the Greek the app shows is checked by program against the text itself: every form in the chapters, every quoted verse at its reference, every parse the drills mark you against. What a program cannot check — whether an explanation is right — is read by a person, chapter by chapter.

Put it on your phone

iPhone or iPad

Open everydaykoine.app in Safari, tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.

Android

Open it in Chrome, tap the menu, then Install app or Add to Home screen.

Computer

It runs in any modern browser. In Chrome or Edge, the install icon in the address bar puts it in a window of its own.

Studying on two devices? Turn on sync in Progress: one device makes a private code and you type it on the other. There is still no account.

Your privacy

No sign-up, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking. Your progress lives on your own device and goes nowhere unless you turn on sync. The details are in the privacy policy.

The textbook it follows

The chapters follow the order of a standard first-year grammar, David Alan Black’s Learn to Read New Testament Greek, so they line up if you are using it — though they are written to teach on their own, and none of the book’s text or exercises is reproduced.
