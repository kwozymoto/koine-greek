Everyday Koine

Learn to read the Greek New Testament, a few minutes at a time. Chapters that teach the grammar, recorded to be read aloud, vocabulary with audio on a schedule that brings each word back just as you are about to forget it, and the whole Greek text with every word parsed.

Open the app

It runs in your browser: no account, no ads, and it works offline once opened.

What is in it

27 chapters

From the letters to reading the text, in short parts with questions along the way. Every chapter will have audio: a voice reads it to you, tables and verses included, lighting each word as it is spoken. About a quarter of the chapters have it so far. What each chapter covers

818 words, with audio

The New Testament’s commonest words, down to about fifteen occurrences — nine words in ten of the running text — each with a recording and an example verse, on a spaced-repetition schedule.

Paradigms you can play

28 reference tables, 20 of them as fill-in-the-grid rounds against the clock, and a parsing drill marked against 1,477 real forms from the text.

The whole Greek New Testament

Every word parsed and glossed on a tap, dimmed or lit by what you already know, plus 12 graded passages and 630 sentence questions drawn from the text.

How a day goes

Open it and Today tells you what to do: the words the schedule has brought back, a few new ones, and the next part of the chapter you are on. Most days that is ten minutes or so. A wrong answer is not a failure — it just brings the word back sooner.

Accurate by construction

An app that teaches a language makes claims about it, and a confident wrong answer is worse than none. So the Greek the app shows is checked by program against the text itself: every form in the chapters, every quoted verse at its reference, every parse the drills mark you against. What a program cannot check — whether an explanation is right — is checked by a person, chapter by chapter.

Your privacy

No sign-up, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking. Your progress lives on your own device and goes nowhere unless you turn on sync. The details are in the privacy policy.

The textbook it follows

The chapters follow the order of a standard first-year grammar, David Alan Black’s Learn to Read New Testament Greek, so they line up if you are using it — though they are written to teach on their own, and none of the book’s text or exercises is reproduced. Everyday Koine is independent, and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the author or the publisher.

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. Everyday Koine is free and will stay free, with no advertising.

Do I need the textbook?

No. The chapters are written to teach on their own. They follow the order of David Alan Black's Learn to Read New Testament Greek, so they line up if you are using it, but none of the book is needed or reproduced.

How accurate is it?

Every piece of Greek the app shows is checked by program against the SBL Greek New Testament: each form in the chapters, each quoted verse at its reference, and each parse the drills mark you against. Explanations are checked by a person. The audio is a synthetic voice and not perfect; where a recording and a lesson disagree, the lesson is right. If you find a mistake, Progress has a problem report that opens an email to us.

Why Erasmian pronunciation, not modern Greek?

It is what most New Testament courses teach, and it keeps letters that modern Greek has merged sounding different from one another, which makes a word easier to spell once you can say it. Chapter 1 explains the choice.

Does it work offline, and where is my progress kept?

Once opened, it works without a connection. Your progress is stored on your own device, and you can export a backup from Progress at any time.

Can I use it on my phone and my computer?

Yes. Turn on sync in Progress: one device makes a private code, and you type it on the others. Your progress then stays the same on each, with no account.

I already know some Greek. Can I skip ahead?

Yes. In Progress, "Studied Greek before?" marks earlier chapters done and brings in the commonest words as familiar, spread over a fortnight.

Is there an Android app?

You can install the web app on an Android phone today: open it in Chrome and choose Install app. A Google Play version is planned for October 2026.

Can a class or a church group use it?

Yes, and please do. Everyone keeps their own progress on their own device, and because the chapters follow a standard first-year grammar, a group can work through them together at the same pace.

Is it still being developed?

Yes. Everyday Koine is updated regularly, and the app tells you when a new version is ready. Ideas and feature requests are welcome at support@everydaykoine.app.
