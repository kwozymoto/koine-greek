## Read this before recording anything

The guide's own second paragraph governs this list:

> This is a procedure, not a formula. Atlas is not deterministic: the same
> `pahss` that once split into "pa has" was later read correctly. Expect
> to produce two or three candidates and choose by ear.

So **every cue here is a candidate that has not been heard.** Two are given
per word, following the guide's step 6 — one spaced, one partly bound. Keep
the shorter one that does not spell an English word, aiming at about 0.5
seconds a syllable.

The **stress** column is the syllable Greek marks with its accent. Atlas has
no stress mark, so it has to fall out of the shape of the cue: do not let an
unstressed syllable carry a long vowel. This is the check guide section 3
asks for, and it is the one a generator cannot do for you.

Vowel length is deliberately *not* guessed. The obvious rule — lengthen the
accented syllable — is contradicted by the shipped clips themselves: λόγος
takes a long `loh` on its accented omicron, and πληρόω takes a short `ro` on
its accented omicron, because the omega after it already supplies an `oh`.
Where a cue below sounds flat, that is the first thing to try.

## What the flags mean

Each is one of the nine documented failure modes, run back over the
generated cue. A flag is a prediction, not a certainty; an empty flag column
means no known failure mode applies, **not** that the cue is right.

| flag | guide | what to do |
|---|---|---|
| `LONG(n beats)` | 4 | Spaces are beats. Five or more and the clip becomes a list of syllables. Use the bound form. |
| `DOUBLED(xx)` | 7 | A doubled consonant can split — `pahss` once became "pa has". If it splits, try the single letter. |
| `SILENT-H-IN-BOUND` | 5 | Closing a space can wake the h in `ah`/`eh`/`oh`. Drop the h in the bound form. |
| `CLUSTER(xx-)` | 8 | English will not begin a word with `pn`/`pt`/`gn` and drops the stop. A light helper vowel forces it: πτωχός → `puh toh koss`. |
| `ALL-LETTER-NAMES` | 3 | Every token is a letter name, so the voice spells it out. `eh pee` → `eppee`. |
| `SPELLS-EN(...)` | 2 | The cue spells an English word and will take its vowels and stress. |

**55 of the 307 carry a flag.** Those are where to start listening; the other
252 are unremarkable by the guide's rules.

cards and examples are all keyed by array position.
